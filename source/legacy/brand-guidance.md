Cave is a software company that finds software solutions for real-life problems. The company was first named Cave Software Solutions and is now registered as **Cave** only (Arabic **كهف**). The brand is built on one idea: a cave offers protection, so Cave provides secure, reliable software environments. Everything here is bilingual. Build every surface in English and Arabic, and treat the Arabic as a first-class version, not a translation afterthought.

## Content fundamentals

- The name is **Cave**, and **كهف** in Arabic. The wordmark is set in capitals (CAVE); the original deck's running copy also uses capitals. Never write "Cave Software Solutions" or "Software Solutions" as part of the name: it was dropped when the company was registered.
- The old descriptor line (SOFTWARE SOLUTIONS / للحلول البرمجية) is retired. Logo files that carry it are not in this system; the original deck in `assets/Reference` and the Illustrator logo files still show it.
- Write plain, declarative sentences that open with the benefit, then the proof. Use the cave, the layers and the river as metaphors, because the mark is built from them. Real examples from the brand deck:
  - "Just as caves offer protection, we provide secure, reliable software environments."
  - "The layered effect symbolizes the depth of knowledge, the stages of problem-solving, and the iterative nature of software development."
  - "Its reflective surface mirrors endless possibilities, emphasizing CAVE's ability to deliver multiple pathways to solve challenges."
- Name the mark's parts as the deck does: **The Cave** (depth and discovery), **The River** (infinite solutions in motion), **Flowing Layers** (iterative development).
- Do not use emoji. The brand has no emoji or decorative glyph language.

## The logo

- Use the logo files in `assets/Logos` as supplied. The wordmark is custom lettering drawn as outlines: never retype it in `display` or `sans`, never redraw the symbol, never recolour its layers.
- Pick the lockup by language and by ground.
  - **Full colour** (`cave-logo-*-color-compact`) goes on `white` or `paper` only.
  - **Reversed, all white** (`cave-logo-*-white-compact`) goes on `teal`, `navy` or `charcoal`. These are the four grounds the deck approves.
  - The lockup is the symbol over the name. **Symbol** alone (`cave-symbol-*`) is for avatars and icons.
- The English wordmark is `navy`. The Arabic wordmark is the deeper `midnight`. Do not swap them.
- English lockups are left-to-right and Arabic lockups are right-to-left, each centred symbol over name. Use the Arabic lockup on Arabic surfaces and the English on English ones.
- Clear space and minimum size are not defined in the deck. Do not invent numbers: keep generous space and ask before setting a minimum size.

## Colour

- **Teal and navy are the brand.** Teal carries agility and adaptability: pivoting and customising solutions for different clients. `navy` carries infrastructure, security and backend stability, "the colour of trust". Build every surface from these two and the neutrals `paper`, `white` and `charcoal`.
- **Teal appears in two values.** `teal` (#179d9f) is the colour of the logo field, the section tabs and the reversed-logo ground. `teal-light` (#51bab6) is the swatch the deck documents with CMYK 65/0/35/0, and the colour of the pattern linework. The deck does not say which is canonical for print, so use `teal` on screen and ask before printing.
- Use the semantic tokens in product: `surface`, `surface-raised`, `ink`, `ink-muted`, `accent`, `accent-text`, `border`, `border-strong`, `focus-ring`. Three themes are defined. **Light** grounds on `paper`. **Dark** grounds on `navy`, which is also the deck's cover colour. **Black** grounds on `charcoal`, the deck's black ground: near-black, never pure `#000`.
- **Text contrast rules.** `teal` is a fill, not a text colour on light grounds: white on `teal` is 3.30:1, so it is allowed only at 24px and above (the section tabs). Under 24px put `on-accent` (`navy`, 4.55:1) on `accent`. Accent-coloured text on light grounds is `accent-text` (`lagoon`).
- **Depth steps.** For ordered series, stages or levels, step through the symbol's layers from outside in: `seafoam`, `teal`, `lagoon`, `deep-teal`, `abyss`. Keep the lit opening `mist` and the river `mint` for highlights. Do not add other hues to a depth scale.
- **Secondary colours** (`slate`, `coral`, `lime`, `magenta`, `plum`, `snow`) are accents for tags, charts and illustration, never for brand surfaces. `slate`, `coral` and `lime` are light: use them as fills with `navy` text. `magenta` and `plum` take white text.
- The brand uses flat colour only. Do not add gradients to the mark, the tabs or the patterns.

## Typography

- **English:** `display` (Hero) is primary, for titles and tabs. `sans` (Poppins) is secondary, for everything read at length. Set body copy in `body`, emphasis in `body-strong`, secondary copy in `body-sm`, and short labels in `label` (capitals, `0.2em` tracking).
- **Arabic:** `ar-display` is Almarai Light, for titles at brand moments, set about 1.18x the size of the English `display` so the two languages look equally light. (Blue Ocean was tried first, but it is far heavier than Hero and made the Arabic look bold beside the English; it stays in the logo lockups and print only.) `ar-text` (Almarai Regular and Bold) is for body copy. Set Arabic text right to left (`dir="rtl"`), start-aligned, and never apply the letter-spacing used by `label`, which breaks Arabic joins.
- Hero has no Arabic. The family stacks already fall back across Latin and Arabic, so use the stack tokens (`--font-display`, `--font-sans`, `--font-ar-display`, `--font-ar-text`) and not a face name.
- Weights available: Poppins Light, Regular, Italic, Medium, SemiBold and Bold; Hero Light and Regular; Almarai Light, Regular, Bold and ExtraBold; Blue Ocean (one weight, logo and print only). Do not request a weight outside these, because browsers fake it. Hero has no bold: emphasise with size, not weight.

## Shape, layers and pattern

- The brand shape is the **rounded triangle**: a soft peak, wide base, generous corners. It appears in the symbol, the three patterns and the letterhead's overlapping corner shapes. Reuse it for avatars, image masks and decorative accents.
- Colour comes in **flat nested layers**, outermost lightest, innermost with the lit opening. Build illustration and backgrounds the same way: stacked flat fields, no outlines, no shadows.
- **Section tabs** are the brand's one piece of layout furniture: a `teal` pill flush to the edge of the page, fully rounded at its free end (`radius-pill`), with the section name in `display` capitals, white. Use the `SectionTag` component.
- Three patterns live in `assets/Patterns`. All sit on `deep-teal` with `teal-light` linework: **outline** (rounded triangles in a staggered grid), **layers** (the symbol tessellated), **dots** (triangle with a centre dot). Use them for covers, edges and full-bleed panels, in one pattern per surface. Never put body text over a pattern.
- On dark covers the outline pattern appears very faint on `navy`, in a tone only slightly lighter than the ground. Keep it that quiet.
- Spacing is a proposed 4px scale. Corner radii beyond the pill (`radius-sm` 8, `radius-control` 10, `radius-md` 14, `radius-lg` 18) are taken from the shipped products, not the deck.

## Iconography

They are drawn in `teal` and, because they are images, cannot take another colour. The deck pairs each with a one-sentence meaning; do the same.

- Social icons on the letterhead are WhatsApp, Facebook, Instagram and Telegram: a `navy` ring with the glyph in `teal-light`. Only the image in `assets/Stationery` exists; redraw them in vector before reuse.

## Product interface

The rules above are the brand. These are the rules for the software built on it (the web console, Cave Shops mobile, Cave POS), and the components in `components/` implement them.

- **Buttons are rectangles.** Use `radius-control` (10px) for buttons, inputs, selects, menu items and nav items. Pills (`radius-pill`) are for badges, chips, avatars and the `SectionTag`, never for actions.
- **One primary action per view.** Primary is `accent-strong` with `on-accent-strong` text (white on `lagoon` in light, navy on `teal` in dark). Brand `teal` stays for marks, tabs and highlights; it is not a button fill and not small text.
- **Cards are flat.** `surface-raised`, a 1px `border`, `radius-md`, no shadow. Only floating layers (menus, toasts, dialogs, sheets) take `shadow-menu` or `shadow-overlay`. Dialogs and sheets use `radius-lg`.
- **Status is never colour alone.** Every status carries a word, and an icon where there is room. Use the `success`, `warning`, `error` and `info` tokens (`-bg`, `-fg`, `-border`, `-solid`); `-solid` is for dots, bars and chart marks, `-fg` for text. Map record states to tones once, in a shared function.
- **Type in product.** Page titles use `page-title` and card or dialog titles `section-title` (Poppins; in Arabic `ar-page-title` and `ar-section-title`, Almarai). Hero (English) and Almarai Light (Arabic) are for brand moments: landing pages, sign-in, splash and empty-state headlines at 24px or more. Tables and forms are always Poppins and Almarai.
- **Arabic names.** In Arabic text the company is **كهف**, the web product **متاجر كهف**, the desktop and mobile till **نقطة مبيع كهف** (never "Cave POS" or "POS"), and the generic term is **نقطة المبيع** ("the point of sale"). Say "تطبيق نقطة مبيع كهف" for the app, and keep the feminine agreement ("نقطة مبيع كهف المكتبية"). Latin product names stay in English text only, in every screen, notification, message, document and screenshot caption.
- **Screenshots.** Marketing screenshots (landing pages, store listings) are captured from the current build, in both languages and all three themes (Light, Dark, Black), with sample data, and are retaken whenever the design changes. A page shows the picture for the visitor's language and the theme on screen, and a theme without its own picture (the shop pages' dark is already near-black) uses the nearest one. Products in the till and the shop page show pictures, never empty placeholders. Screenshot tabs that turn by themselves stop for good once the visitor picks one.
- **The Cave account.** One account serves Cave Shops and Cave POS (and later products), so account screens (sign in, register, forgot and reset password, account settings, link a device, "Sign in with Cave") say **Cave** (Arabic **كهف**, the account **حساب كهف**) and show the symbol, set in `display` or `ar-display`. They name the products only in one context line ("One account for Cave Shops and Cave POS"). Product screens (a console, a storefront, navigation) keep the product's name. Page titles, share previews and the copyright line on account screens say Cave. Use the `AuthCard` component.
- **Themes.** Light and Dark are the brand's own; Black is for people who prefer a near-black screen. Offer Light, Dark, Black and System. System follows the device: a light device gets Light; a dark device gets the **platform's default dark theme**, which is Dark unless an administrator sets Black in Platform settings (`defaultDarkTheme` in `appConfig/public`, read by the web, mobile and desktop apps and cached on each device so it is right offline and on first paint). Someone who picks Light, Dark or Black themselves always keeps that choice; the setting never overrides it. Never derive Black from Dark by darkening: use the `black` values in `tokens.json`. In Black, text on `teal` fills (`on-accent-strong`, `on-accent`, `on-danger`) is `charcoal`, which reads better there than navy (5.1:1 against 4.55:1). Everything else, including the status and chart colours, passes on `charcoal` unchanged. The white logo lockup is approved on `charcoal`.
- **Right-to-left.** Build with logical properties (start, end, inline) so one layout mirrors. Numbers, prices, phone numbers, emails, codes and URLs stay left-to-right inside Arabic text (`cv-ltr`), aligned to the start edge. Mirror directional icons (chevrons, arrows, back) but not logos, checks or media controls.
- **Touch and density.** Controls are 40px on desktop and 48px on touch; minimum touch target 44px. Tables use `body-sm` and 12px vertical cell padding.
- **Motion.** `duration-fast` (150ms) for hover and focus, `duration-base` (200ms) for overlays, `easing-standard`. With reduced motion, drop transitions and keep loops to a slow pulse.
- **No gradients, no emoji.** The flat-colour rule holds in product. Replace purple or teal gradient banners with flat `accent-soft` or `surface-sunken` fields.
- **Icons.** One rounded line set, 1.75px stroke, 20px (24px in navigation on touch), in `ink`, `ink-muted` or `accent-text`. The three brand icons in `assets/Icons` are for marketing and empty states, not UI chrome.
- **Third-party colours.** WhatsApp green (`#25D366`), Facebook, Instagram, Telegram and Google marks keep their own colours as marks only. Put the label in `ink` beside them; do not use them as button fills. The one exception is the shop's "Order on WhatsApp" button (see Shop colours).
- **Shop colours.** A shop's own colour (storefront, and the app's accent for a shop that has the feature) is free: Cave does not restrict the hue. What Cave guarantees is that everything drawn from it stays readable, by role, computed from the colour rather than chosen by the shop:
  - **Fill** (buttons, bars, badges): the shop colour itself, with text on it of whichever of `white` or `charcoal` reads better, at 4.5:1 or more. Where neither reaches 4.5:1 (mid colours near a relative luminance of 0.2, such as many blues and reds), nudge the fill's lightness by the smallest step, keeping its hue, until one does.
  - **Colour as text or an icon** (links, prices, outlines): a version of the shop colour moved in lightness only (darker on a light surface, lighter on a dark one) until it reaches 4.5:1 for text and 3:1 for icons and outlines on that surface. Never use the raw colour as small text.
  - **Tints** (selected rows, soft badges): the shop colour at low alpha over the surface, with the text colour from the line above.
  - A colour too close to its surface to be seen as a fill (under 3:1) gets a `border-strong` outline.
  - Shops cannot change `ink`, the surfaces, the status tokens, `focus-ring` or third-party marks: "paid", "error" and the WhatsApp mark keep their own colours. The "Order on WhatsApp" button is the mark's green (`#25D366`) with `charcoal` text (8.7:1), never white text (1.98:1).
  - The storefront's dark ground follows the visitor's Cave theme: `navy` and `gray-800` in Dark, `charcoal` and its raised surface in Black (the platform's default dark theme decides when the visitor follows their device); light is `paper` and `white`. The shop's colour is made readable on whichever ground is shown. The visitor's own light or dark choice follows their device by default and is remembered on that device.
  - The same rule applies wherever a shop colour is printed or shared: QR posters, invoices, receipts and share cards.
  - **Discounted prices.** A plan can carry a regular price beside the price people pay (100 struck through, then 3): the regular price in `ink-muted`, struck through and smaller, the price now large, the currency and cycle beside it, and one chip with the percent off. The price charged is always the price now. Prices on the landing, the subscription pickers and the apps follow the same pattern. Never show a regular price that is not above the price.
- **Add-ons and plans.** Every feature and every quantity (branches, products, customers, orders, storage, team seats, image slots) can be an **add-on**: a feature add-on switches features on, an extra-room add-on adds a number of one allowance per unit. A **plan** is made of add-ons: it lists the add-ons it includes (`includedAddons`), and the plan's own features stay the source of truth for what a shop gets. There is no separate "package": every purchasable thing is a plan, and a plan is shown as what it is made of. The rules, on every platform:
  - Add-ons live in one place, the owner's **Add-ons** page (web console, Cave Shops mobile, and Settings > Subscription in Cave POS), owner only. It shows what is running (with its end date and a **Renew** action), then what is on sale for the shop's plan, then the order. Use the `AddonCard` and its order bar.
  - A card that cannot be bought says why in one sentence; it is never hidden and never a disabled button with no reason. An add-on needs what it builds on (a funnel needs analytics): it says so instead of switching the missing feature on behind the shop's back. The plan's own features are never bought twice.
  - The price is per unit, per cycle. The server prices every order and creates the payment; the screen only shows the total first. Whish is first, cash is the fallback an admin confirms, a discount code applies to the whole order, and an order that costs nothing activates at once.
  - Each add-on runs for its own period and ends on its own date. When it ends the shop returns to what its plan includes. Nothing a shop built is deleted when an add-on ends.
  - Admins manage the catalog (name, kind, what it grants, price, regular price, requirements, plans it is limited to, on sale or retired) and list the add-ons a plan is made of, on the web and on mobile. Retiring an add-on stops new sales and leaves existing purchases running.
  - Names, descriptions and units have an Arabic field; an Arabic screen shows the Arabic one.
  - **Plan artwork.** A plan can carry one picture (`imageUrl`, an absolute https address, set in Admin > Plans, optional). It is a **photograph**, 3:2 (1536 x 1024), in the Cave grade: warm light, teal and natural wood, real people at work in a real shop, café or restaurant, the screen shown in the scene being a phone, tablet or till (never invented UI text). No text or logo in the picture: names, prices and sentences are drawn by the app, so Arabic and English share one file. It is shown **full width at 3:2, cropped to fill** (`object-fit: cover`, anchored a little above centre, because the subject sits in the upper middle), flush to the top of the card or as one half of the large plan panel, never inside a small letterboxed box. Keep faces and the device away from the outer 10% so crops at 16:7 (the desktop till window) still work. A plan without a picture shows its card as before; a failed download leaves the card whole. Add-on artwork is different: small illustrations that sit in a fixed 48 x 40 slot, contained, so lines without one still line up.
  - **Plan details ("See everything").** One view, the same on every platform: a dialog on desktop and a full-screen sheet on a phone. It shows the price, then **Included add-ons** as a grid of pictures (two columns on a phone, three on desktop) grouped by kind in a fixed order (Selling, Orders, Restaurant, Team, Room, Branding, Insights, AI, More), each with its picture, name, a "+3 branches" chip for extra room, a one-line description and its price on its own; an add-on with no picture shows its kind's icon on the tinted tile so the grid stays even. A line says what the add-ons alone are worth bought separately. Then **Room and limits** as a two-column table (products, customers, orders a month, team seats, branches, pictures per product, storage in MB or GB, history), leaving out what the plan does not include, then one line: need more, add any add-on. Buttons: Browse add-ons, Close, and Choose {plan} (the same label as the card's button). **Every feature is an add-on**: a plan's features are never listed as text, they are the add-ons it is made of (the basics are the Online shop page and Orders on WhatsApp add-ons), so a plan is its add-ons plus its limits. An add-on that is no longer on sale is skipped.
- **Plans on the landing.** Plans are chosen with tabs (name and price), one **short panel** at a time, not a wall of cards: the plan's photo on one side (stacked above on a phone), then its name, one sentence, the price (regular price struck through, price now, cycle, percent off), **three or four short lines** (the first may be a caption such as "Everything in Shop, plus:"), one primary button, "Or try everything free for a month" as a quiet second button (the trial is a button, never a card), and **See everything in {plan}**. The popular plan is selected first. The business tier ("Contact us") and the free desktop till are two quiet strips under the panel. Plans a shop has bought or been given privately are never listed. The card never lists features as text and never lists add-ons: both live in the details.
- **Product logos.** Each product's footer shows its own logo file (Cave Shops and Cave POS each have one) so a product can get its own mark without touching the code. Cave pages carry it; a shop's own page never does.
- **Powered by Cave.** Every product says it comes from Cave with one line, "Powered by [Cave mark] Cave" (Arabic: "مدعوم من [mark] كهف"), in the footer of the web pages, under the sign-in cards, at the foot of the Cave POS sidebar. The mark and the name are one link to www.cave.technology. Same design on every platform; see `components/PoweredByCave`.
- **Footer.** A product's footer is one centred stack: its own logo, Powered by Cave, the copyright line, and up to five links. The reference is the Cave POS website's; see `components/Footer`.
- **Choosing the colour** (Website > Look, shop settings, on every platform): ready-made colours with Cave's own first, a colour-code field and a free picker, and a reset to Cave's colour. Beside it, a small storefront drawn from the derived roles on a light page and on a dark page, so the owner sees what customers see. One plain sentence says what was done: it reads well, buttons got a small shade change, or buttons got an outline. Show the contrast numbers for button text and coloured text. A locked plan shows the same controls disabled with one line saying which plan unlocks them. Never reject a colour; adjust and explain.
  - **Preview while editing** (web Website tab): the storefront itself, framed at phone, tablet or desktop width and in the light or dark page, updated as the owner types, so a change is seen before it is saved or published.
- **Charts.** A single series uses `chart-1`. Ordered series (stages, levels, depth) step through the depth ramp (`seafoam`, `teal`, `lagoon`, `deep-teal`, `abyss`). Unordered series take `chart-1` to `chart-4` in order, at most four, with direct labels and the values in a table or tooltip; group the rest as Other. Status series (paid, owed, overdue) use the `*-solid` status tokens, not the chart colours. Never rely on colour alone: label the series.
- **Tutorial videos.** Cave's YouTube tutorials all follow one style, made by `tool/tutorial_video` in the Cave POS repository (its `STYLE.md` is the rulebook and `build.py <slug> lint` checks it). One task per video, 1 to 2 minutes: intro (logo, Hero title, one line), an overview picture, numbered steps, an optional "Good to know" with up to three icon cards, and an outro that points to the live plans, the website and **Powered by Cave**. A video never states a price, a plan name or a limit (they change, the video does not); it says "part of selected plans, or add it to any plan" and sends people to the live plans page. Only real app screens, in a monitor frame with the Footer's shadow, on `surface` with `ink`, `ink-muted`, `lagoon` actions and one `teal` highlight at a time; Hero for the display title and Poppins for everything else (Almarai Light at 1.18 times for Arabic). Sound has three layers: a clear voice, a soft music bed about 11 dB under it, and small cues (a pop, a tick, a chime), mastered to -16 LUFS. Every video ships with a voice-only copy, a 1280x720 thumbnail (teal split, product badge, a three-line Hero title, one white chip, the real screen), captions and upload notes. The list of titles is `VIDEOS.md`, and the prompt to ask for a new video (a topic, or none to take the next title) is `PROMPT.md`, also shown in the web console under Admin > Platform settings. Titles read "Product Tutorial: what you get" in 70 characters or fewer. The channel is one company channel, **Cave**, with one playlist per product (Cave POS, Cave Shops) plus Getting started and Tips and tricks; Arabic videos are separate and use the Arabic names.
